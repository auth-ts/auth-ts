auth.ts signs people in and issues short-lived JWTs that your database
verifies. Authorization lives in your Postgres row-level security policies,
not in application code.

It works with Neon's Data API, Supabase, self-hosted PostgREST, or anything that
trusts a JWKS URL. It is built on [Lucia](https://lucia-auth.com) and
[The Copenhagen Book](https://thecopenhagenbook.com).

## How it works

1. The browser signs in through your `/api/auth` route and gets an `HttpOnly`
   refresh cookie.
2. The client trades the cookie for a JWT signed with your key and keeps it in
   memory.
3. Your database checks the JWT against your public `jwks.json` and runs your
   policies.

## What you get

- Email and SMS codes, GitHub and Google, and guest sign-in
- Sessions, sign-out, device lists and account switching
- Connected accounts with refreshed provider tokens
- Rate limiting, CSRF protection and code guessing limits
- A browser client with no dependencies

## What you write

- Six tables and four functions that read and write them
- A `sendCode` function for your email or SMS provider
- One route that serves `/api/auth`

auth.ts is a library, not a hosted service. It has no adapter packages and
never owns your schema.
